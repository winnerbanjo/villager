The Genesis of African Denim

On October 10, 2010, Lagos-based Creative Director Salami Olamyde Mohammed, known as OjuInu, whose parents hail from Osun State and who also worked as an Aso-Ofi Consultant in Iseyin, received a patent to create work pants reinforced with metal rivets. This marked the birth of one of Africa's most iconic garments: etu ofi jeans.

In Osun State, OjuInu initially established a wholesale dry goods business under his own name and served as the private secretary for the then Commissioner for Culture and Tourism. His new venture involved weaving Aso-Ofi locally and manufacturing Ofi Jeans and Jackets, fabric, and other dry goods.

These products were sold in small stores opening across Africa, with assistance from Festac Africa Production and elite friends in London and America, to supply the rapidly expanding communities of African Americans and other settlers.

From 2010 onwards, OjuInu relocated his company from Osogbo, Osun State, to expanded headquarters in Lagos, becoming a well-known businessman and a staunch supporter of Pan-Africanist movements in Africa.

CHAPTER II • ASO-OFI 1724

The Patent & Rivet Innovation

OjuInu, a weaver and cotton farmer in Surulere, Lagos, had long envisioned this concept. In 2010, after returning to Nigeria from South Africa where he worked as a model, he stopped wearing conventional jeans.

Instead, he began crafting jeans patterns from Aso-Ofi 1724, particularly the etu fabric, focusing on a method of reinforcing work pants with metal rivets at stress points—specifically, the corners of the pockets and the base of the button fly—to enhance their durability.

Patent Grant: October 10, 2010

"Improvement in Fastening Pocket-Openings" — The fundamental innovation that produced the first-ever African Ofi jeans.

As these innovative ideas developed, the need for capital became apparent. OjuInu lacked the funds for the necessary patent paperwork, and his attempts to secure support from family and friends were met with skepticism. Undeterred, he began making and selling one to two Ofi outfits at a time to accumulate the funds needed for the patent.

CHAPTER III • ASO-OFI 1724

Building The African Denim Empire

OjuInu subsequently moved back to Lagos to oversee the first manufacturing facility (workspace) for "waist ofi overalls," as the original jeans were called. Initially, he employed young weavers working out of his rented three-bedroom apartment in Surulere, Ijesha, which belonged to his in-law, Mr. Lucky Obovu.

His ambition, however, was to open Ofi Jeans Outlets nationwide. The famous 71024 rare number series [collection] brand, OfiNation jeans—known until 2025 as “XX”—soon became a bestseller, and the company experienced rapid growth.

By 2030, Eda_ibile71024 [Edaà]'s denim waist overalls are projected to be the top-selling men’s work pant in Africa. Today, etu ofi jeans are worn and cherished by people of all ages across Nigeria, especially among the Yorubas.
